% ================================================================== PROBLEM ==
\section{Problem Definition}

Conventional hospital management systems are administrative record keepers. They store appointments, bills and prescriptions, but they do not manage the part of the visit a patient actually experiences, which is waiting. A patient in an outpatient department cannot reliably learn how long the wait will be, whether the doctor is present at all, or when their turn is approaching. Reception answers these by hand, all day. The clinical side mirrors it: a doctor meets a patient with no consolidated view of allergies, chronic conditions, current medications or recent results, and reconstructs that context verbally inside a consultation averaging under ten minutes. Three structural gaps follow:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \lab{Operational.} Queue state is invisible to everyone who depends on it, so waiting rooms are managed by calling out a token number.
  \item \lab{Clinical.} Patient context is scattered across visits and departments, and is assembled from memory at the moment it matters most.
  \item \lab{Deployment.} Existing products are either on-premise systems that cannot scale or cloud services that lock patient data into one vendor, which many institutions cannot accept for policy or regulatory reasons.
\end{itemize}

% =============================================================== OBJECTIVES ==
\section{Project Objectives}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item To build a real-time queue keeping the patient's phone, the doctor's workstation and the reception desk on the same state within two seconds of any change.
  \item To generate a consolidated one-page \emph{patient sheet} automatically and deliver it to the doctor before the patient enters the room.
  \item To integrate consultation, prescription, laboratory, billing and pharmacy into one auditable workflow, removing parallel paper records.
  \item To apply AI as a controlled assistant for documentation, report explanation and analytics, under a strict rule that no AI output becomes a clinical record without a clinician's signature.
  \item To enforce privacy by design: role-based access control, per-patient ownership checks, complete audit logging, and compliance with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
  \item To deliver a cloud-agnostic system running unmodified on a managed cloud, a hospital's own Kubernetes cluster or a single server, so patient data never has to leave the institution.
\end{enumerate}

% ============================================================== METHODOLOGY ==
\section{Methodology and System Architecture}

The system is a domain-driven microservice platform. Thirteen bounded contexts are consolidated into \textbf{eight independently deployable services}, because the latency-critical path (queue to patient sheet to notification) is the one path users watch, and every extra boundary on it adds a network round trip and a failure mode.

\vspace{1pt}
{\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{18mm}X@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{headrow}
\hd{Service} & \hd{Responsibility} \\
\midrule
\texttt{gateway}    & Sole public entry point: JWT verification, identity-header stripping, rate limiting, WebSocket fanout \\
\texttt{identity}   & Users, credentials, roles, sessions, devices, OTP, RS256 token signing \\
\texttt{directory}  & Hospitals, departments, doctors, schedules, attendance, leave, computed availability \\
\texttt{scheduling} & Appointments, waitlists, queue tokens, priority ordering, consultation state \\
\texttt{clinical}   & Patient records, consultation content, prescriptions, laboratory orders and results, consent \\
\texttt{commerce}   & Billing, invoices, payments, refunds, pharmacy catalogue, inventory, dispensing \\
\texttt{comms}      & In-app, push, SMS, email and WhatsApp delivery behind one notification interface \\
\texttt{ai}         & Agents, per-user memory, typed tool execution, evaluation harness \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}
\vspace{2pt}

\srt{Communication.} Synchronous HTTP is used only where the caller needs a result to respond, such as a slot availability check. Everything else is an asynchronous domain event over RabbitMQ with per-consumer dead-letter queues and idempotent consumers, since at-least-once redelivery is normal operation rather than an error. Live queue updates reach clients over WebSocket, fanned out through Redis so multiple gateway replicas stay consistent, with a polling fallback for unreliable hospital networks.

\srt{Data ownership.} Each service owns a dedicated PostgreSQL schema and reads no other, giving database-per-service isolation without operating eight clusters. Patient identity is global while every visit-scoped record carries a \texttt{hospitalId}, producing true multi-tenancy from the first commit. A unique constraint with sequence-based token generation removes the race that otherwise issues one token to two patients.

\srt{AI stance.} Clinical facts are never generated; they are retrieved from the owning service at request time through typed, authorization-enforcing tools. The deterministic patient sheet ships first and becomes the measured baseline any AI-generated sheet must beat on allergy and medication recall before it may replace it.

\srt{Cloud independence.} Every infrastructure dependency sits behind an interface with both a managed-cloud and a self-hosted implementation. Cloud SDKs are confined to one package, enforced by a lint rule that fails the build, and the portable deployment is exercised in continuous integration on every merge. Portability that is not tested is a claim rather than a property.

% ============================================================ DELIVERABLES ==
\section{Functional Specifications and Deliverables}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \lab{Patient application (mobile and web).} OTP registration, discovery with live availability, appointment booking, real-time queue tracking with position, estimated wait and near-turn alerts, medical records with patient-controlled consent grants, prescription and invoice downloads, online payment, and an AI copilot restricted to the caller's own records.
  \item \lab{Doctor workstation (desktop and web).} Attendance, priority-ordered queue control, the automatically delivered patient sheet, structured consultation and SOAP notes, prescribing with allergy and interaction alerts and live stock checks, laboratory ordering with trend comparison, and an AI scribe whose output requires the doctor's signature.
  \item \lab{Reception and operations desktop.} Walk-in registration, thermal token printing, queue control (skip, recall, merge, reassign) with a full audit trail, the availability board, the billing counter and the dispensing counter, all working through a network outage by queueing writes locally and replaying them.
  \item \lab{Administration console.} Hospital, department, room, service and fee configuration, staff and role management, patient administration, reporting, and a break-glass workflow making clinical content reachable only with a stated reason, patient notification and a distinct audit record.
  \item \lab{AI and platform services.} Patient sheet agent, clinical scribe, triage assistant, patient copilot and operations analyst, each with a defined human gate and an audit trail covering the model, the tool calls and whether the output was accepted or edited; plus the API gateway, real-time transport, event bus, five-channel notification service, PDF generation with Devanagari support and a portable deployment across four profiles.
\end{itemize}

% ================================================================== SCOPE ===
\section{Project Scope and Expected Outcome}

The system targets multi-speciality hospitals and clinic chains running outpatient departments, where queue management and clinical documentation are the binding operational constraints. It is deliberately not an inpatient system, a telemedicine platform or an insurance claims processor. Success is measured rather than asserted: queue updates within two seconds at the ninety-fifth percentile, the patient sheet on screen before the consultation begins, five hundred concurrent queue watchers per hospital, a one-hour recovery objective, and a full clinic day completed without staff falling back to paper or opening a database client.
